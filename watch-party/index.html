Directions and parking

Getting here.

The watch party is in Room 3241 of the Roy F. Mitte Building (RFM) on the Texas State University campus in San Marcos. Visitors should park in the LBJ Student Center Parking Garage and walk to the building’s front entrance.

1. Park at the LBJ Student Center Parking Garage

This is a pay-to-park garage. Take a ticket as you drive in, then pay at the kiosk before you return to your car. The kiosks take credit and debit cards and cash (no bills larger than $20). Parking currently costs $3 for the first hour and $6 for up to two hours. See current garage rates.

2. Walk to the Roy F. Mitte Building

From the garage, follow the route on the map to the front entrance of the Roy F. Mitte Building at 749 N. Comanche St. The walk takes about 8 minutes.

3. Find Room 3241

Come in through the front doors. Take the staircase in the lobby up one floor, then walk forward until you reach Room 3241.

Why 5:33 in the morning?

To catch the International Space Station, a rocket has to lift off at the moment Earth’s rotation carries the launch pad underneath the station’s orbit. That moment comes about once a day, and on October 13 it falls before sunrise in Texas.

It also means there is no room for delay on the day. If the rocket can’t launch on time, the attempt moves to another day.

The mission

About PROVES-Maia.

Mission page →

PROVES-Maia is a CubeSat, a miniature cube-shaped satellite, assembled by Space Lab in the Department of Physics. It is Texas State University’s first spacecraft.

The satellite is Texas State’s contribution to the Pleiades Five Program, a collaboration between Cal Poly Pomona, Columbia University, Northeastern University, UC Santa Cruz, and Texas State University. Each university builds its own satellite around a shared spacecraft design created at Cal Poly Pomona. Our team assembled the spacecraft and added a scientific payload of our own, which includes a gamma-ray radiation detector designed and built by TXST alumnus Stephan Cope.

PROVES-Maia is flying on CRS SpX-35, a NASA cargo resupply mission to the International Space Station (ISS), with launch services provided through NASA’s CubeSat Launch Initiative. Once PROVES-Maia reaches the ISS, it will be stored there for about two weeks before it is deployed into orbit around the Earth.

If the launch is delayed.

Rocket launches are often rescheduled for weather or technical reasons, sometimes in the last minutes of the countdown. Before you leave home, check the launch status at the top of this page or on Instagram at @txstspacelab.

Good to know

Questions and answers.

Who can come?

Everyone. The watch party is open to Texas State students, faculty, and staff, and to the public.

Will we see the rocket from San Marcos?

No. The rocket launches from Cape Canaveral, Florida, about 1,000 miles away. We’ll watch the live broadcast together on screen.

Can I bring children?

Yes! Children are welcome with an adult.

Does it cost anything? Do I need to register?

This event is free and there is no need to RSVP. However, parking in the LBJ Student Center Parking Garage is paid if you plan on parking there.

How long will it last?

The launch itself is quick: the spacecraft is in orbit about ten minutes after liftoff. Expect to be finished by about 6:00 AM.

Will there be food or coffee?

There will be free coffee and snacks provided at this event.
